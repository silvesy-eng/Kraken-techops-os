THE IDEA

TechOps should be visible as a system.

The team should not have to choose between being responsive today and getting better tomorrow. This concept makes both visible: live service demand, incidents, planned technical change, security obligations, team capacity and continuous improvement — in one operating rhythm.

02 · OPERATING MODEL

Lean by design. Disciplined where it matters.

Enough structure to scale safely — without recreating heavyweight enterprise IT.

01

Stay in the work

Leaders retain hands-on exposure to tickets, incidents and user friction so priorities are grounded in reality.

02

Make demand visible

Separate interrupts from planned work. Review age, impact and recurrence — not just queue volume.

03

Automate toil, not judgement

Automate repetitive provisioning, evidence capture and routing. Keep humans where context and risk matter.

04

Security × productivity

Treat security and user experience as a joint design problem, not competing objectives owned by different teams.

05

Global guardrails, regional ownership

Standardise identity, device posture, incident handling and controls while allowing sensible regional execution.

06

Close the loop

Every meaningful incident, repeated ticket theme or failed change should produce learning, ownership and a visible follow-up.

OPERATING RHYTHM

A cadence small teams can actually sustain.

The goal is not more meetings. It is fewer surprises, cleaner ownership and protected improvement capacity.

Daily10-minute demand + incident pulseWhat changed? What is blocked? What needs escalation?

03 · INCIDENTS

Calm response. Clear ownership. No theatre.

A lightweight incident model that protects users, keeps stakeholders informed and converts disruption into learning.

DETECT

Establish reality before solving.

Confirm who is affected, business impact, scope, security implications and whether the failure is still expanding.

  • Assign one accountable incident lead
  • Capture known / unknown / next evidence
  • Set severity from impact, not emotion
04 · PROJECT DELIVERY

Technical change without losing the people.

For identity, MDM, authentication, office and SaaS migrations, adoption quality matters as much as technical completion.

05 · FIRST 90 DAYS

Understand → stabilise → improve.

I would resist arriving with a pre-baked transformation. First understand where the real constraints are.

0–30Understand the system
  • Work the queue and join live incidents
  • Map services, ownership, vendors and risk
  • Baseline demand, ageing, interrupt load and project commitments
  • 1:1s with TechOps, Security, UK/APAC leaders and key partners
  • Identify immediate reliability or security exposures
31–60Protect focus
  • Introduce a visible capacity and prioritisation rhythm
  • Clarify incident roles, severity and communications
  • Select top recurring toil / ticket drivers
  • Agree regional guardrails vs global standards
  • Build skill matrix and development goals with the team
61–90Improve deliberately
  • Automate one high-volume, low-judgement workflow
  • Remove one structural cause of repeated incidents
  • Publish a lightweight TechOps service health view
  • Sequence the next two quarters of technical projects
  • Make ownership and operational follow-through boringly reliable
06 · WHY I BUILT THIS

I like roles where leadership and configuration detail coexist.

The Kraken brief stood out because it does not treat leadership as distance from the work. That is how I have operated across international support environments: lead people, improve the system, manage incidents and still get close enough to the tooling and workflow to understand the real problem.

My background is broader than conventional internal IT, which is useful here. I have led multi-region technical support teams, improved service performance, built operational workflows, worked across SaaS platforms and integrations, handled high-stakes escalations, and repeatedly focused on reducing friction rather than adding process for its own sake.

People leadershipLed and developed international technical support teams across UK, EMEA, APAC and India.
Service managementBuilt SLA, capacity, escalation, knowledge and operational models; ITIL Foundation.
Hands-on mindsetComfortable in Salesforce, Zendesk, APIs, Postman, automation tooling and workflow configuration.
Incident leadershipOwned escalations, customer communications and operational recovery in public-safety and transport technology.
Scale + consistencyImproved support performance while expanding teams, regions, workflows and partner coverage.
Challenge conventionI prefer the smallest amount of process that reliably improves the outcome.
THE GAP I WOULD NOT HIDE

I would need to deepen some internal-IT platform specifics quickly.

I would not pretend to have run every Kraken-listed tool in production. My approach would be to combine strong service/incident fundamentals and hands-on technical curiosity with focused ramp-up in the exact IAM, MDM, endpoint security and network stack in use. The role explicitly values learning — this is where I would apply it.
